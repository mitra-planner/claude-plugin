# Wedding planning guide

Help the user plan a wedding with concrete, sequenced advice. This skill works without any account or connection.

## Start by getting the basics

Ask only for what you don't already know, in one short message:

- Date (or season) and how flexible it is
- City or region, and whether it's a destination wedding
- Rough guest count
- Total budget and currency
- Number of days and events (single day vs. multi-day)
- Who is helping: couple only, family, a professional planner

Do not invent any of these. If the user doesn't know yet, plan around ranges and say so.

## Order of decisions

Work in this order, because each step constrains the next:

1. **Budget and guest count**: they drive venue, catering and vendor choices.
2. **Date and venue**: book these first; good venues go 9–18 months ahead for peak dates, sooner for off-peak.
3. **Core vendors**: photographer, caterer (if not included), decor, music/DJ, planner or coordinator.
4. **Guest list and invitations**: send save-the-dates 6–9 months ahead (earlier for destination weddings), formal invitations 6–8 weeks ahead.
5. **Details**: attire, stationery, favours, transport, accommodation blocks.
6. **Final month**: confirm every vendor in writing, finalise the day-of timeline, seating, final headcount to the caterer.

## Budget

- Allocate by category and keep an unallocated buffer of roughly 5–10%.
- Typical share of spend, as a starting point to adjust, not a rule: venue and catering are the largest (often around half combined), then photography/video, decor and florals, music, attire, stationery and everything else.
- Track quotes separately from committed spend; compare quotes in the same currency.
- Per-guest costs scale the budget fastest. Fixing the guest count early is the highest-leverage budget decision.

## Multi-day weddings

- Treat each event as its own mini-plan: its own guest subset, venue, timing, dress code, decor, catering and vendors.
- Mark which guests are invited to which events; a flat "invited / not invited" list breaks down quickly.
- Build one master timeline that shows travel and buffer time between events, then a detailed run-of-show per event.
- Plan guest accommodation and transport early: they are the most common source of day-of stress.

## Guest list

- Start from the maximum list, then tier it (must-have, would-like, if-space) so cuts are easy later.
- Expect roughly 75–90% of invitees to attend for a local wedding, lower for destination weddings. Use that for the caterer's estimate, then replace it with real RSVPs.
- Collect dietary needs and plus-one details in the RSVP, not afterwards.

## Vendors

- Get at least three quotes for the big-ticket categories, with the same scope written down for each.
- Ask for: what's included, overtime rates, cancellation and payment schedule, backup plan.
- Keep deposits and due dates in one place.
